Career and income

What employers test in 2026

Several 2026 interview guides agree on the same short list. They are mostly published by training companies, so treat their percentages with caution, but the pattern is consistent.

  • Window functions (ROW_NUMBER, RANK, LAG, running totals) are named as the most-tested advanced skill for analysts.
  • Joins, especially LEFT JOIN with NULLs to find missing rows.
  • CTEs alongside window functions, for example removing duplicates with ROW_NUMBER.
  • Aggregation traps: WHERE versus HAVING, NULL handling, double counting after joins.
  • Most questions are rated easy or medium. Writing queries from scratch under time pressure is the best preparation.

Sources: weekday.works, techinterview.org. One site's percentage breakdown looked garbled, so none are quoted here.

Pay in South Africa: a rough picture

These are estimates from salary aggregator sites, not official statistics, and they disagree with each other.

  • One site gives an average data analyst salary of about R47 500 a month (about R570 000 a year), with entry level near R20 000 a month and senior roles up to about R80 000 a month (source).
  • PayScale's figures for analysts who list SQL skills show entry level near R203 000 to R214 000 a year, mid-career about R464 000 to R485 000, and experienced about R616 000 to R641 000 (example page).
  • The same sites list SQL, Excel, a BI tool (Power BI or Tableau) and Python or R as the usual skill set. "High demand" claims are not backed by job-market data in those pages.

Unverified: check recent job adverts and a South African salary survey before you rely on any number.

Passive income: an honest view

Nothing found in the research gave reliable data for SQL or data-science products specifically. What the general sources agree on:

  • It is not passive at first. Templates, courses and downloads take months of building and marketing before sales, and income is uneven (one freelancer's account).
  • The market is crowded. There are thousands of general data courses. A narrow audience, such as "SQL for small shops" or "SQL reports for farmers", is easier to reach than "learn SQL" (data analyst product ideas).
  • Freelance reporting is active income but is the more reliable first step, and good freelance work can turn into a template or course later.

Realistic routes, in order of effort

  1. Freelance reporting for small businesses: weekly sales or stock reports from their data.
  2. Query and template packs for one industry (retail, farming, clinics), sold as downloads.
  3. Power BI or spreadsheet dashboard templates built on top of SQL.
  4. A small course or practice set for one niche, once you have taught people in person.

Unverified: no income figure is claimed here. Test a small product first and measure the sales yourself.
