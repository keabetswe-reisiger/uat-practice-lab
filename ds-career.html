Career and income

How to turn the lessons into a job, freelance work or a small income stream. The advice here comes from published guides and is general. Salary and earnings figures vary a lot between sources, so use them as rough ranges only. The Research page lists what was checked and what was not.

Roles you can aim for

RoleWhat you doCore skillsStart with
Data analystAnswer business questions from existing data, build reports and dashboards.SQL, spreadsheets, charts, basic statisticsSQL lessons and the Pivot lab

Roles overlap and titles differ between companies. Many people start as analysts and move towards modelling or engineering.

How to get real experience

  1. Work through the modules in order, pass each checkpoint at 80%, and repeat the lab tasks until the answers feel routine.
  2. Use your own coffee business data. Real data you understand beats a downloaded dataset you do not.
  3. Complete two small projects from the list below and write each up in plain English with one chart and one recommendation.
  4. Publish the write-ups where an employer can see them, such as a free code-hosting page or a personal site.
  5. Take the mock exam regularly until you score 80% or more twice in a row.
  6. Apply for analyst or junior roles, internships and learnerships. Offer a small paid or unpaid project for a local business to gain a reference.

Passive income: an honest view

There is no such thing as effort-free income. The numbers below come from marketing-style blogs and anecdotes, so they show the shape of the opportunity, not a promise. Medians matter more than the headline numbers.

RouteWhat it involvesWhat sources suggestMain caveat
Online courseRecord and publish a course on a learning platform.One guide reports a median instructor income under $500 a year, and about $100 to $500 a month for a new course after several months.The data science course market is crowded. You need a clear niche, such as data for small shops in South Africa.
TemplatesDashboard or spreadsheet templates sold on a marketplace.Guides cite from tens to a couple of thousand pounds or dollars a month for well-run shops after 3 to 12 months of steady work.Top figures are the best cases. The median seller earns far less.
Datasets and reportsClean, documented datasets or niche market reports.No reliable earnings figures were found.Only worth it if the data is truly yours to sell, legal to share and not personal information.
Freelance projectsPaid analysis, dashboards or SQL help.No reliable rate survey was found in the research.This is active work, not passive. It is the usual way to earn first and learn what people pay for.
Writing and tutorialsBlog, newsletter or video tutorials that build an audience.Slow to start. Sources describe months of effort before income.Success depends on audience and consistency.

A sensible order: learn the skills, build a portfolio, earn through a job or freelance work, and only then package what you know into a product. Selling SQL or data skills as a product works best when you have already solved the same problem for paying clients.

SQL and data science skills can also support a coffee business directly: better stock ordering, pricing and staffing. That return is real even before any product income.

Money and the South African market

Published South African salary figures conflict widely. One source gives a junior data scientist around R30 000 a month and another around R574 000 a year, and a salary aggregator gave a far lower annual figure from only 101 reports. Treat all of them as unverified. Check current adverts for the roles you want and ask people working in them. Knowing the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) is repeatedly described as a plus for data roles, though no survey data was found to prove that.
